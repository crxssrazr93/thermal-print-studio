          How to install a font

          Copy the file into ~/.local/share/fonts and run fc-cache -f. On Arch and CachyOS a packaged font is sudo pacman -S ttf-jetbrains-mono; on Debian and Ubuntu sudo apt install fonts-jetbrains-mono; on Fedora sudo dnf install jetbrains-mono-fonts.

          Devices

            Add a device

            What the printer can do, in the escpos-printer-db schema. If yours is not listed, describe it: the head width is the part that has to be right.

            What the roll measures. 58 and 80 are the usual sizes.

            How much of that the head can actually print, in dots. It is always less than the paper: a 58 mm roll usually prints 48 mm of itself.

            What it can do

            Anything the printer cannot do in firmware is drawn into the picture instead, which is slower and softer but always works.

            How to talk to it

            Receipt printers speak ESC/POS. The label printers sold beside them usually speak TSPL, and one sent ESC/POS prints a label of characters.

            If pictures come out as pages of characters, the firmware does not know GS v 0. Column mode is the older way and nearly everything understands it.

            These four decide whether a long page prints or streaks. A printer that drops the bottom half of everything is being fed faster than it can burn: smaller writes, a longer pause.

            The head and the cutter are not in the same place, so the last lines have to be fed past the blade. Zero feeds three lines instead.

            More heat makes the same dots blacker. More darkness, in the page settings, makes more dots black and costs the fine detail: reach for heat first.

            Some printers want a reset, a character set or a last feed that the firmware forgets. Datasheets write these in hex, so they are taken in hex.

            Paper

            –

            The roll is wider than the head. With this on, the preview is drawn at the width of the paper and the part the printer can actually reach is outlined inside it, so the margin is visible before the paper goes through rather than after.

            What goes on the wire

            Anything a printer would be sent: a .prn capture from another app, or bytes from a datasheet. Spaces and 0x are ignored.

              Network

              –

              Off, the server listens only to this machine and nothing else can print. On, anything that can reach the port can print, spend your paper and read your presets: there is no password. Turn it on for a network you trust, and off again when you are done.

              Port 9100 is what every print system means by a network printer. With this on, CUPS on another machine, a till, or anything that speaks ESC/POS can print through this app to a printer that has no network of its own. The bytes are passed through untouched, under the same lock the app's own jobs take. It follows the setting above: local only until this app is exposed.
